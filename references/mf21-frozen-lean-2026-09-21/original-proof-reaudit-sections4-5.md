**The inverse-kernel citation is accurate.** I independently read the original [Böttcher–Widom preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0412269). Its formula (5), printed page 2, is the kernel in manuscript (27), with the stated reflection. Printed page 3 defines the clamped ceiling index; printed page 4, immediately before (13), states the $L^\infty([0,1]^2)$ convergence for the exact $b=1$ symbol used here. Thus this manuscript did not substitute operator-norm convergence for the stronger kernel convergence needed by its argument.

**The diagonal sentence at line 335 is valid for these kernels.** Let $K_n$ be the prescribed matrix-entry step kernel and let $e_n=\|K_n-G_m\|_{L^\infty}$. Within any diagonal grid square, $K_n$ has one fixed value. The full-measure set where the error is at most $e_n+\varepsilon$ is dense in the interior of that square. Approach any assigned diagonal point, including its cell boundary, through this set. Continuity of $G_m$ gives the same bound at that point; then let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The clamped endpoint convention also places the endpoint in the closure of a positive-area cell. Integrating gives $n^{-2m}\operatorname{tr}(A_n^{-1})\to\int G_m(x,x)\,dx$. Cell constancy and continuity, both explicitly present in the manuscript, supply the needed premise.

There is nevertheless a real distinction between the written and formal proof organization here. The target therefore does not literally depend on an abstract $L^\infty$-restriction-to-diagonal lemma.
