# Simple determinant zero: statement lock

For a differentiable complex square matrix curve, a zero of its determinant
with nonzero first derivative has a one-dimensional complex kernel. Prove
positivity of the kernel dimension from singularity, and exclude dimension
at least two using the proved determinant derivative theorem.

Apply this to the manuscript's actual boundary matrix curve and the proved
linear equivalence with the Toeplitz eigenspace. Under a valid distinct
characteristic root list at that point, a simple boundary-determinant zero
therefore has a one-dimensional Toeplitz eigenspace.

This is the geometric-multiplicity argument in Lemma 4. It is a consequence
of an actual nonzero determinant derivative, not a proof that the normalized
spectral roots have such a derivative. Analytic estimates and the symmetric
matrix algebraic-multiplicity bridge remain separate obligations.
