# Analytic division by the endpoint coordinate: statement lock

For an actual analytic function `f : ℝ → ℂ` near zero, prove that
`dslope f 0` is analytic at zero. The function equals `(f(t)-f(0))/t`
off zero and the actual derivative at zero. The proof must derive its
analytic extension by the order-one analytic Taylor theorem, not assume
regularity of the divided function.

If f is analytic at every real point, show `dslope f 0` is analytic at
every real point. The nonzero points follow by ordinary division.

This supplies the manuscript's removable simple-zero factors near (18).
It does not yet assert any normalized Vandermonde quotient is nonzero or
bounded; the root tangents and actual denominator factors must supply that.
