## Mathematical findings

Theorem 1.3 passes. Exact arithmetic checks confirm the expansion of
$`b=h^2`$, the factorization
$`z^2h'(z)=(2z+101)((z-1)^2+100)`$, the three critical values and the two
endpoint factorizations. The comparison polynomial has two roots of modulus
10 throughout the parameter interval, including its endpoints. The strict
Rouche estimate $`32991<40000`$ therefore gives exactly two roots in
$`|z|<30`$ and excludes boundary roots.

On the open parameter interval, the two simple roots admit consistent
real-analytic labels by continuation along an interval. Both branches extend
to the same two endpoint critical points, remain injective and have no other
intersection. The local holomorphic square coordinate at each simple critical
point makes their union a regular real-analytic arc there. The harmonic
maximum/minimum principle and open mapping theorem force the resulting
Jordan curve to surround zero. Its image under $`h`$ is exactly
$`\mathrm{i}[-2000,2000]`$, giving $`b(\gamma)=[-4000000,0]`$.

At $`\lambda_0=L^2`$, the factorization
$`z(h(z)-L)=(z+97)(z^2-\rho^2)`$ and the exact signs of
$`q(z)=z(h(z)+L)`$ put the latter's roots in
$`(-97,-30)`$, $`(-30,-\rho)`$ and $`(0,\rho)`$.
All six roots are simple, and $`\pm\rho`$ occupy modulus ranks 2 and 3.
The pole order is 2, so these are the required Schmidt-Spitzer ranks.
The implicit-root differentiation yields

```math
F'(\lambda_0)=-\frac{97}{2L\rho(97^2-\rho^2)}<0.
```

The inverse image of the unit circle under the locally biholomorphic ratio
$`F=u/v`$ is therefore exactly the stated regular arc with nonzero imaginary
tangent. This proves nonreal limiting spectral points without relying on
the plotted finite spectra or an invalid finite-matrix spectral-mapping step.

Proposition 4.1 passes independently. Exact coefficient checks give
$`a(z)=\mathrm{i}h(-\mathrm{i}z)`$ and

```math
z(a(z)-\mathrm{i}L)=-\mathrm{i}(z^2+\rho^2)(z+97\mathrm{i}).
```

The rotated curve has the required reality and winding properties.
The distinct roots $`\pm\mathrm{i}\rho,-97\mathrm{i}`$ satisfy
$`10<\rho<11<97`$, so the first two modulus ranks coincide. Since the pole
order is 1, the nonreal point $`\mathrm{i}L`$ belongs to the limiting spectrum.

## Sources and scope

The equal-modulus criterion agrees with
[Shapiro-Stampach, arXiv v4, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.00741v4)
and [Duits-Kuijlaars, equations (1.9)-(1.10)](https://arxiv.org/html/0704.0378).
The correction appended to Shapiro-Stampach, printed p. 27, explicitly leaves
the weaker limiting-spectrum implication conjectural.
[Giandinoto, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16266v1) concerns the
necessary-direction generalization for real matrix-valued symbols.
Ahlfors's third edition, Chapter 4, Section 3.3, Corollary 2, p. 132, supplies
the local conformal-inverse result used at the endpoints.

If every finite-section spectrum were real, every point of the limiting
spectrum would be real. Thus the new counterexamples imply finite-section
failure as claimed. SP-06's original statement, canonical path, references,
prior audit notes, existing proof credit and Lean verification section are
unchanged. No existing Lean certificate is asserted to cover the new result.
The strengthening retains the conservative `Solution claimed` evidence level,
now accompanied by this informal audit; the entry's `Lean verified` status
continues to describe the original finite-section target.
